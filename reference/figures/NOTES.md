# REACT paper figure candidates

Generated by `experiments/paper_figures.py` from `outputs/final/`, the final run of 2026-09-30.

Unless a figure says otherwise, every number below is for Eindhoven at 4.2 nats with floor A (1.02 cm), using the climatology margin.

Terms used throughout:
- **Recovery, censoring and relapse are "given onset"**: they are measured only over events where the forecaster fell behind.
- **KM** is the Kaplan–Meier estimate. Its tail ("not recovered by 15 s") treats windows that end at the next event as censored. The raw "censored" share counts them as failures, so it is the larger of the two.

All plotted statistics were checked against the run summaries, and an independent review reproduced them for all 336 groups at 4.2 nats across the four runs: anticipation, censoring, relapse, relapse3 and KM medians match exactly.

Further method notes:
- **"Recovered within K frames"** is 1 minus the KM curve at K, so windows that end before K are handled correctly.
- **Oracle B is evaluated on every event.** The models are evaluated on 2,318–2,560 of the 2,560 Eindhoven events, depending on lead, because some forecasts are missing at the start of a track.
- **Relapse uses the three-frame rule:** three consecutive frames with P(I<0) ≥ 0.95, starting within 10 frames of recovery, measured as a share of recoveries. The relapse window is cut short at the end of the event window.

## The narrative the figures support

Learned models (the two GRUs and Trajectron++) lead on classical metrics everywhere. Their error at each lead is up to 7 cm lower and their NLL 0.6–2 nats lower than the best kinematic model. The exception is Day in the Life at lead 1, where the error is tied and NLL is 0.3 nats worse.

Under REACT, that advantage is spread unevenly. It is concentrated in some phases of recovery, some leads, some precision targets and one dataset, and in other places it reverses. Each of these gaps would matter for a physical AI system:

- **Early recovery is the most consistent gap.** At leads 10 and 15, the best learned model recovers within 10 frames less often than the best kinematic model in every dataset × target setting (8 of 8, −2 to −24 percentage points). It is largest on Eindhoven at leads 10 and 15: after falling behind, the learned models recover within 10 frames on 25–30% of events, against 47–51% for CV and IMM. They catch up by 30 frames, and they then have fewer unrecovered events. The early window is exactly where a planner needs the forecast to be useful.
- **Trajectron++ and its sharp mixture.** It anticipates the most events at lead 1 (44%). But when it does fall behind at lead 1, it is censored on 22% of those events, against 4% for the best kinematic model and 2% for the GRUs.
- **The 0.1 m target removes most of the learned advantage,** and Trajectron++ falls behind even the kinematic models:
  - Eindhoven, lead 10: Trajectron++ is censored on 51% of onsets, against 36% for the best kinematic model and 33% for the GRUs.
  - Eindhoven, lead 15: 68% against 57% and 59%.
  - Day in the Life, lead 10: Trajectron++ recovers within 15 frames on 23% of onsets, against 34% kinematic and 32% GRU.
  - Day in the Life, lead 15: Trajectron++ is censored on 81%, against 72% kinematic and 59% GRU. Its three-frame relapse rate is 26%, against 20% kinematic.
- **Day in the Life under the climatology margin** is where the learned advantage is broadest. Trajectron++ still loses early recovery there at leads 10 and 15 (43% and 34% within 10 frames, against 47% and 40% for the best kinematic model). At lead 5 it is censored on 19% of onsets, against 2% for the GRUs.

A practical reading: kinematic models stay competitive because their advantage sits in the first frames after a change. That is when a robot planner is most exposed, and it is invisible to ADE, FDE and NLL.

## Relapse: real rates and the "under 1%" figure

On real data the three-frame relapse rate is 3.5–74% on Eindhoven and 2–100% on Day in the Life, as a share of recoveries.

The "under 1%" figure is the rule's **false**-relapse rate in the known-truth simulation: the share of events where the forecaster was truly still ahead after recovery but the rule flagged a relapse. With exp10's 10-frame window, fig11c measures it at:
- 0.1% without a noise floor;
- 0.0% with the floor.

So the high real-data rates are real setbacks, not artefacts of the test.
